Glossary of Marine Lubrication Terms

Short, direct-answer definitions of common marine lubrication terms.

BN (Base Number)
A measure of a lubricant's ability to neutralize acidic combustion by-products, expressed in mg KOH/g. Higher BN oils are typically used with higher-sulphur fuels.
TBN (Total Base Number)
Another term for Base Number, used interchangeably in marine lubricant specifications.
TPEO (Trunk Piston Engine Oil)
A lubricant designed for medium-speed trunk piston diesel engines, used in generators and some propulsion systems.
EAL (Environmentally Acceptable Lubricant)
A lubricant formulated to be biodegradable and minimally toxic, used at oil-to-sea interfaces such as stern tubes to meet environmental discharge regulations.
MARPOL Annex VI
The International Maritime Organization (IMO) regulation limiting air pollution from ships, including sulphur oxide emissions, which directly affects lubricant selection.
IMO Tier II/III
Emission standards set by the IMO for marine diesel engines, influencing which lubricants are compatible with newer, lower-emission engine designs.
Viscosity Grade (SAE/ISO)
A classification indicating a lubricant's thickness and flow characteristics at specific temperatures, used to match oil to engine and equipment requirements.
